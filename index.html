History & Culture

Journey Through Siquijor’s History, Traditions, and Legends

  • The First Parish

    In 1783, the first parish in Siquijor was established by Spanish missionaries, marking the beginning of the island’s rich religious and colonial history. The parish shaped the culture and traditions of the local community, with its church standing as a testament to centuries of faith and resilience.

  • Dilaab Festival

    Named after the Visayan word for "glow" or "blaze," it symbolizes the island’s legendary “Island of Fire” moniker. The festival features cultural performances, street dancing, and ceremonies honoring St. Francis of Assisi, the island’s patron saint. It’s a time when locals and visitors alike come together to experience the magic and warmth of Siquijor.

  • Solili Festival

    The Solili Festival is a lively cultural event celebrated in Siquijor, showcasing the traditional Solili dance, a courtship ritual performed by newlyweds. It is a joyful occasion that symbolizes love, unity, and the strong community bonds of the Siquijodnons.

  • Bolo Bolo Folkhealing

    Bolo-Bolo is a traditional healing ritual in Siquijor, practiced by local healers known as "mananambal." Using a glass of water, a black stone, and a bamboo straw, the healer blows into the water while chanting prayers, believed to remove negative energies and ailments.
